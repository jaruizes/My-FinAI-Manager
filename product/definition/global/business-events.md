# My-FinAI-Manager — Business Events

## Purpose

This document defines the main business events that occur within or around My-FinAI-Manager and describes how those events may affect business information, functional domains, and product processes.

The model is inspired by Event Storming, but it represents the consolidated business view rather than a technical event-driven architecture.

The events described here are business facts or relevant external occurrences.

They do **not** imply:

- Kafka;
- a message broker;
- asynchronous communication;
- event sourcing;
- an event schema;
- an integration contract;
- a distributed architecture;
- or any specific implementation mechanism.

Technical communication and integration choices belong to architecture and feature implementation.

---

# Business Events Principles

The business events follows these principles:

1. Events describe something that has already happened or has been observed.
2. Event names should normally use past tense.
3. Business events should be meaningful to the product domain.
4. External events may influence the product without being generated by My-FinAI-Manager.
5. Derived analytical events represent conclusions produced by analysis rather than externally observed facts.
6. An event may affect one or more business information objects.
7. An event may trigger a business process or reassessment.
8. A business event does not imply a technical message or broker event.
9. Feature Definitions decide which events are relevant to a concrete capability.
10. Technical event contracts, topics, schemas, delivery semantics, and integration mechanisms are defined separately.

# Event Categories

My-FinAI-Manager distinguishes three main categories of events.

## Domain Events

Events representing significant facts that occur inside the product domain.

Examples:

- PortfolioCreated
- PositionAdded
- PositionIncreased
- PositionReduced
- PositionRemoved
- PortfolioValued
- RiskDetected
- RecommendationGenerated
- StopLossRecommendationGenerated
- PortfolioReviewCompleted

These events describe meaningful changes or outcomes inside My-FinAI-Manager.

---

## External Events

Events that occur outside My-FinAI-Manager but may affect portfolios or analysis.

Examples:

- MarketPriceObserved
- EarningsPublished
- DividendAnnounced
- GuidanceChanged
- RatingChanged
- RegulationAnnounced
- InterestRateChanged
- MacroeconomicIndicatorPublished
- NewsPublished

These events originate from the external world and may or may not be relevant to a specific Portfolio.

---

## Derived Analytical Events

Events representing conclusions obtained by analysing available information.

Examples:

- RelevantInformationDetected
- PortfolioImpactDetected
- ConcentrationRiskDetected
- RiskLevelChanged
- InvestmentThesisWeakened
- InvestmentThesisStrengthened
- StopLossReassessmentRequired
- RecommendationReassessmentRequired

These events are not equivalent to external facts.

They represent analytical conclusions and should retain their supporting Evidence and level of confidence where applicable.

# Business Requests and Policies

Event Storming commonly distinguishes between something that is requested and something that has actually happened.

This document uses the following conceptual convention:

```text
Intent / Request
      ↓
Business Rule / Policy
      ↓
Event
```

For example:

```text
Update Portfolio
      ↓
Validate requested change
      ↓
PositionIncreased
```

Requests are not events because they may fail or be rejected.

Examples of business requests include:

- CreatePortfolio
- AddPosition
- IncreasePosition
- ReducePosition
- RemovePosition
- RequestPortfolioValuation
- RequestPortfolioReview
- ReassessRisk
- GenerateRecommendation
- ReassessStopLoss

The precise commands, APIs, interfaces, or technical representations are not defined here.

# Event-to-Entity Relationship Principles

Events may affect business objects in different ways.

## State-changing Events

They modify the current business state.

Examples:

- PortfolioCreated
- PositionAdded
- PositionIncreased
- PositionReduced
- PositionRemoved
- InvestmentThesisUpdated

---

## Observation Events

They introduce new external information without directly changing the Portfolio.

Examples:

- MarketPriceObserved
- EarningsPublished
- NewsPublished
- MacroeconomicIndicatorPublished

---

## Analytical Events

They introduce a derived conclusion.

Examples:

- RelevantInformationDetected
- PortfolioImpactDetected
- RiskDetected
- InvestmentThesisWeakened

---

## Decision-Support Events

They produce advisory information for the Investor.

Examples:

- RecommendationGenerated
- StopLossRecommendationGenerated
- PortfolioReviewCompleted
- AlertGenerated

This classification helps preserve an important distinction:

```text
What happened externally
        ≠
What the Portfolio currently contains
        ≠
What My-FinAI-Manager inferred
        ≠
What My-FinAI-Manager recommends
```

# Event Provenance and Time

Business events are time-dependent.

Where relevant, an event should conceptually preserve:

- Event Time — when the represented occurrence happened;
- Observed At — when My-FinAI-Manager became aware of it;
- Source — where the information originated;
- Confidence or Verification Status — when the event is not a directly verified internal fact.

The exact technical representation is not defined by this document.

External events and derived analytical events should preserve sufficient provenance to explain later why a Portfolio Review, Risk, or Recommendation changed.

# Relationship with Technical Event-Driven Architecture

This event model is a **business model**, not an integration architecture.

For example:

```text
PositionAdded
```

means:

> A new Position has been added to a Portfolio and this fact is relevant to the business.

It does not determine whether the implementation uses:

- a direct function call;
- an in-process domain event;
- a database transaction;
- a workflow engine;
- Kafka;
- another messaging system.

If the architecture later decides that a business event must be externally published, then the technical design should define:

- event contract;
- schema;
- topic or channel;
- producer;
- consumers;
- compatibility rules;
- delivery semantics;
- versioning;
- observability.

Those concerns belong to architecture, ADRs, and feature implementation planning.

# Business Events Evolution

This business events represents the current high-level understanding of My-FinAI-Manager.

New Feature Definitions may:

- introduce new business events;
- refine existing event meaning;
- reveal additional reactions;
- introduce new business processes;
- change which domains or information objects are affected.

Changes must preserve the distinction between:

- externally observed facts;
- internal domain facts;
- derived analytical conclusions;
- recommendations.

Business events should not be added merely because an implementation happens to publish a technical message.
